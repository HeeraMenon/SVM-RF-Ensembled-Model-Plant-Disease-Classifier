AIM
---
The project aims to put forward an enhanced model that classifies diseases in the tea plant by processing the image of it’s leaf. We intent to utilize the concept of ensemble model of machine learning algorithms to bring advanced results in classification and to use CNN-based feature extraction methods for better image processing.


OBJECTIVES
-----------
- To create and introduce a unique model of plant disease classification  and an efficient. 
- To ensemble SVM and RF – two prominently used classifiers – to merge their gains and compensate for each others drawbacks.
- To achieve higher accuracy and scores (precision, recall, F-1) as compared to already proposed models. 
- To contribute to the ongoing research of ML-based plant disease classification.

CONCLUSION
-----------
In summary, our proposed model leverages the strengths of SVM and RF classifiers alongside MobileNetV2 for efficient disease detection in tea leaves. By combining these classifiers through a stacking ensemble approach, we achieved a validation accuracy of approximately 98%, outperforming existing models in the literature. Our model demonstrates the potential for future enhancements, including extending classification to more specific infections and incorporating live image capture for user convenience.


FUTURE SCOPE 
------------
We would suggest a few changes in the future scope of our system, having demonstrated that our model has successfully brought forth the best aspects of Support Vector Machine and Random Forest models to classify diseases in tea leaves in experiments. 

Some pointers on this are: 
- The dataset can extended to more number of diseases and for different plant(s). For this alterations can be made to the model for smooth performance
- Live capture of input image can help reduce time of action in crop failure prevention   
- A good database of these diseases and it’s leaf image indicators can ensure the availability of proper fertilizers/pesticides for each disease and help prevent crop failure
- Incorporating appropriate storage and detection technologies can make it a vital tool for crop producers across the globe
